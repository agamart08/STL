Common questions

Do I legally need a fire risk assessment?

If you are responsible for non-domestic premises, or for the shared parts of an HMO or block of flats, yes. The Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 requires one. Failing to comply can lead to enforcement action, unlimited fines and, in serious cases, prison.

How long does the visit take?

A small shop or flat block often takes a couple of hours on site. Larger or more complex buildings take longer. I'll give you a realistic time when I quote.

What should I have ready on the day?

Any previous fire risk assessment, floor plans if you have them, and your alarm, emergency lighting and extinguisher service records. If you don't have some of these, that's fine. It simply goes into the report.

How often does it need reviewing?

There is no single legal date, but the assessment must be kept up to date. Review it whenever something changes, such as the layout, occupancy or use of the building, and commonly at least once a year.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the size, number of floors and complexity of the building. Send me the address and the type of premises and I'll give you a fixed price.
